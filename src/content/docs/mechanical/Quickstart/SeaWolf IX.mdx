*Written by Alexis Mead*

**It’s a bird! It’s a plane! No, it’s SWIX!**

SeaWolf IX is the latest iteration in our line of robots. You’ll never guess, but the other ones were also named SeaWolf. Wild coincidence, right? Anyways, SWIX was physically put together for the first time in March of 2025 and competed for the first time in RoboSub 2026. At this first competition, SWIX could only manage movement, as it had just been turned on for the first time at competition. In the coming years, SWIX will have fully controlled movement and peripherals to complete additional tasks.

![SeaWolf IX with its hulls, thrusters, frame, and buoyancy boxes](/mechanical/quickstart/seawolf-ix.jpg)

*Fig. 1 SeaWolf IX*

The biggest parts of SWIX are the hulls: the main hull and the battery hull. The main hull stores all of the electronics that SWIX needs to run (or swim, I guess). The battery hull holds the batteries (GASP no way). Both of these components obviously need to stay dry (hence the sealed hulls) or else things go bad (shorting or fire), so these hulls are tested every time before they go in the water. There are also two camera hulls on the robot: one looking forward and one looking down. These let the robot do image detection and other neat stuff (this is software magic; they do cool things with the cameras).

There are eight thrusters mounted around the robot. With the positions they are in, they allow the robot to move with six degrees of freedom. Being able to move anywhere within the underwater environment allows the robot to easily navigate locomotion tasks (like Gate and Slalom) and full rotation capabilities are essential for turning (duh) and for balance corrections underwater. Part of that balance relies on the buoyancy of the robot. To easily adjust the weight distribution, buoyancy boxes were designed and attached to each leg of the robot. Small fishing weights (not lead ones, being slowly poisoned is bad, I’m told) can be added or removed from each box as needed.

There will be other cool mechanisms added to the robot soon, like a dropper, which drops small balls into bins. Even further on, there will be a grabber arm mounted on the bottom of the robot to grab and place items. SWIX has an excellent future, and I can’t wait to see where we take this robot!
